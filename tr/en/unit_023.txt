STZ-3	"STZ-3 was designed at the Stalingrad Tractor Factory, and was the first Soviet agricultural tractor which had no basis on foreign designs. Almost 200,000 models were produced between 1937 and 1952, nearly all of them after the war. During the war, it was regularly pressed into military service whenever the dedicated artillery tractors were unavailable. "
STZ-5	"STZ-5 was the upgraded artillery tractor version of the civilian STZ-3. Around 3500 were produced at Stalingrad Tractor Factory before June 1941, and nearly 10,000 were completed before the Battle of Stalingrad, making it the most numerous Soviet artillery tractor of the war. Its nickname means 'Follower of Stalin'."
Komintern	"Komintern (portmanteau of Communist International) was a powerful gun tractor capable of towing virtually any artillery piece in Soviet service, including the super-heavy 203mm howitzer. It was replaced in production by the even heavier Voroshilovets in 1940, yet there were still 1500 in service in June 1941, and around 500 were left by the end of the war."
AS 604(r)	"Komintern (portmanteau of Communist International) was a powerful gun tractor capable of towing virtually any artillery piece in Soviet service, including the super-heavy 203mm howitzer. Hundreds were lost by the end of 1941, with some redeployed in German service under the designation Artillerie-Schlepper 604(r)."
T-20 Komsomolets	"Komsomolets ('Communist Youth') was an armoured artillery tractor, developed in the mid-1930s alongside amphibitious tankettes. While it was only designed to tow light anti-tank guns and short 76mm guns, its use had dramatically improved their response time. Nearly 8000 were produced between 1937 and late 1941, when its production lines were devoted to what would become the T-60."
T-20 APC	"T-20 Komsomolets was an armoured artillery tractor, developed in the mid-1930s alongside the Soviet amphibious tankettes. Nearly 8000 were made between 1937 and late 1941, when their production was halted in favour of light tanks. After the German invasion, many were also used as an improvised APC for small teams."
T-20 (FIN)	"T-20 Komsomolets was an armoured artillery tractor, developed in the mid-1930s alongside the Soviet amphibitious tankettes, and armed with a single machine gun. Nearly 8000 were made before production ended in late 1941, and some were lost to the Finnish forces during the Winter War and the Continuation War."
AS 630(r)	"T-20 Komsomolets was an armoured artillery tractor, developed in the mid-1930s alongside the Soviet amphibitious tankettes, and armed with a single machine gun. Nearly 8000 were made before production ended in late 1941. Many units ended up captured by the Germans during the early war, and were redeployed as Artillerie-Schlepper 630(r)."
T-20 (ROM)	"T-20 Komsomolets was an armoured artillery tractor, developed in the mid-1930s alongside the Soviet amphibitious tankettes, and armed with a single machine gun. T-26T	"This was a gun tractor version of the T-26 tank. While the project began in the early 1930s, it was soon outproduced by Komsomolets and only 217 tanks were converted in total, with the vast majority lost by 1942."

Voroshilovets	"Voroshilovets, meaning 'follower of Voroshilov', was the most powerful wartime Soviet gun tractor. It could tow any artillery piece, and at a higher speed than its predecessor. As with the KV tanks, its name referred to the People's Commissar of Defense Kliment Voroshilov. 1100 were produced before its Kharkov factory had to be abandoned in August 1941."
AS 607(r)	"Some Voroshilovets gun tractors were captured and redeployed in German service as Artillerie-Schlepper 607(r)."
GAZ-60	"After the three-axle versions of GAZ-AA and ZIS-5 proved insufficiently off-road capable to serve as artillery tractors, half-track conversions were attempted instead. GAZ-AA's already low carrying capability of 1.5 tons was further diminished on GAZ-60 due to the extra weight of tracks, so few vehicles were made."
ZIS-22	"After the three-axle versions of GAZ-AA and ZIS-5 proved insufficiently off-road capable to serve as artillery tractors, half-track conversions were attempted instead. ZIS-22 was first deployed during the Winter War after only a short development time, with predictable results: its nickname plays on words for 'go anywhere' off-road vehicles and means 'Will bog anywhere'."
ZIS-33	"The ZIS-33 was developed after Nikita Khrushchev observed Soviet trucks struggling in off-road conditions during the Liberation Campaign. The track was mounted directly onto an unmodified ZIS-5 rear axle to save on cost and to speed up deployment. Around 4,500 were made in six months before production ceased due to its extreme unreliability and high fuel consumption rate, with many converted back to ZIS-5s."
ZIS-42	"The first attempts to convert the ZIS-5 into a medium half-track artillery tractor had performed very poorly during the Winter War, but all attempts to develop a a more powerful truck chassis were cut short by the German invasion. ZIS-42 represented a substantial improvement over its half-track predecessors, but the initial 1942 production run of a few hundred models was rushed and had reliability issues."
ZIS-42M	"ZIS-42M was the final model in a series of half-track conversions of the ZIS-5 truck. More reliable and fitted with a stronger engine, it could adequately tow all but the heaviest guns. Around 6000 were produced between 1943 and 1946."
ZIS-5 (GER)	"This 3-ton truck is the workhorse of the Soviet armed forces.The Germans preferred to rely on their own or on captured French trucks during the first months of the invasion, but those vehicles struggled with the cold and generally couldn't use the locally abundant diesel fuel, so ZIS-5 trucks were redeployed in German service."
