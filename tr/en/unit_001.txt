Panzer I A	"The Panzer I was first produced in the early 1930s, and was intended to serve as a training vehicle until heavier tanks could be produced. The A variant was the first model, and gave useful experience in tank-building to German industry. Most Panzers I A had been converted to the Panzer I B standard by 1939."
Panzer I B	"The Panzer I was first produced in the early 1930s, and was intended to serve as a training vehicle until heavier tanks could be produced. The B variant was slightly larger, heavier, and more powerful than the earlier A variant, and its chassis was used for a number of different vehicles."
Panzer I F	"The Panzer I F had very little to do with earlier models. Work began in 1938 with the aim of creating a heavily armoured tank which would be deployed to support infantry storming heavily fortified lines. It soon became clear that MG-only designs were obsolete, and only 30 vehicles were produced in 1942, with the majority used for anti-partisan operations. A few were also deployed around Kursk."
Sd. Kfz. 265	"This modification of a Panzer I was designed as a vehicle for higher-ranking officers in the Panzer divisions. A turret was replaced by a larger superstructure in order to fit a more powerful radio station, which also limits the vehicle to a single machine gun. 190 were deployed during the invasion of Poland, where it was soon found to be insufficiently armoured for its purpose."
Sd. Kfz. 265 (late)	"The original Sd. Kfz. 265 modified a Panzer I hull to serve as an armoured vehicle with a high-powered radio for senior officers in Panzer divisions. After substantial losses during the invasion of Poland, the superstructure of surviving vehicles was uparmoured at the cost of mobility. After 1941, the few vehicles which survived were handed over to infantry division commanders."
Panzer II A	"First designed in 1934, the autocannon-equipped Panzer II was always intended to be a stop-gap solution until proper medium tanks could be designed and built. Even so, it has proved to be a highly capable light tank. This initial version entered production in 1937 and it composed the bulk of all German tank forces during the early period of the war. "
Panzer II A (late)	"Some Panzer II A models were later upgraded with additional armour on the front of the turret and hull."
Panzer II D/E	"The Panzer II D had additional hull armour compared to the A variant. This unit also represents the very similar E variant of the Panzer II. Around 50 tanks of both variants were deployed in Poland and France in 1939 and 1940. All surviving tanks were later converted into Marder tank destroyers or flame thrower tanks."
Flammpanzer II	"Based on the D and E variants of the Panzer II, the Flammpanzer II was armed with two remotely controlled flame throwers mounted on trackguards on each side. A smaller turret without the 20mm main gun was used for this flame thrower tank variant. About 90 Flammpanzer II tanks were deployed in Russia in 1941."
Panzer II F	"The Panzer II F represents a later version of the Panzer II with heavier hull and turret front armour."
Panzer II J	"The Panzer II J was an experimental light tank design. Intended to be a heavy reconnaissance vehicle, it had much thicker armour than the other Panzer II variants. However, the improved protection significantly reduced the mobility of the vehicle. Only about two dozen tanks of this type were ever built and were only deployed on the Eastern Front."
Panzer II L	"The Panzer II L 'Luchs' or 'Lynx' was a major redesign of the model, and was intended to serve in an armoured reconnaissance role."
Flammpanzer 38(t)	"This was a rare flamethrower variant of the Jagdpanzer 38(t) 'Hetzer' tank destroyer. Only 20 vehicles were modified in this manner, and they were deployed during the Battle of the Bulge and in Hungary."
Panzer III D	"First designed in 1934, the Panzer III was intended to be the main battle tank of the German armoured forces. The D model was a prototype model and only a very small number ever entered service. Its most unusual feature is its twin co-axial MGs."
Panzer III F	"The model F of the Panzer III was the first model to enter mass production. Unlike the majority of gun tanks, it has two co-axial machine guns. It was intended to serve the main battle tank of the German armoured forces in 1939, but supplies remained limited until 1940, and by 1941, it was outnumbered by the more advanced models."
Panzer III G	"The model G of the Panzer III mounts a medium-length 5cm main gun, and has additional front turret armour compared to the earlier model F."
Panzer III H	"This model is similar to the late model G of the Panzer III, but it has additional bolt-on armour plates added to the hull."
Panzer III J	"This variant of the Panzer III with a long 5cm gun was rolled out at the end of 1941, and saw action in North Africa as well as in Russia."
Panzer III L	"This Panzer III variant mounts a long-barrelled 5cm KwK L/60 gun, and has additional armour compared to the H variant."
Panzer III M	"The Panzer III M had additional armour compared to the L variant. Its hull side was reinforced with external armour plates."
Panzer III N	"The N variant was a late attempt to extend the service life of the Panzer III, armed with the same low-velocity 7.5cm gun as the early Panzer IV and StuG variants. Its main task is to provide infantry support."
Panzer III N (late)	"The N variant was armed with the same low-velocity 7.5cm gun as the early Panzer IV and StuG variants in an attempt to extend its service life in an infantry support role. Due to larger shells taking up more space, it was more likely to burn then the earlier variants. The last 167 Panzer IIIs were built on the M variant chassis between February and August 1943."
Flammpanzer III M	"The Panzer III M had additional armour compared to the L variant, and its hull sides were reinforced with exterior armour plates. This rare variant replaces the main gun with a flamethrower. 100 were built in early 1943."
Panzer IV A	"Designed in the mid-1930s as an infantry support tank with a stronger HE shell and lower ground pressure than the Panzer III, the Panzer IV was eventually upgunned to become the main battle tank of the wartime German forces. This first production model had an underpowered engine, and only 35 were produced."
Panzer IV C	"Designed in the mid-1930s as an infantry support tank with a stronger HE shell and lower ground pressure than the Panzer III, the Panzer IV was eventually upgunned to become the main battle tank of the wartime German forces. Models B and C had an upgraded engine and armour, but deleted the bow machine gun and stored less ammo. 177 were produced prior to the invasion of Poland."
Panzer IV D	"Originally intended to be a support tank, the Panzer IV was adapted to serve as the main battle tank of the German forces during the war. First built in 1937, the A to F types of the tank were armed with a short gun and designed to support infantry."
Panzer IV E	"The E variant was similar to the D variant of the Panzer IV, with the notable addition of appliqué armour on the hull and turret."
Panzer IV F1	"The early F variant was designed with single-plate armour, heavier than earlier variants, and required wider tracks to carry the increased weight."
Panzer IV F2	"The F2 variant was the first Panzer IV variant equipped with the long-barrelled 7.5 cm KwK 40 L/43 gun. It was a transitional model with small production numbers, and was soon replaced with more heavily-armoured variants mounting the same gun."
Panzer IV G	"Produced from May 1942, the G variant was produced in greater numbers than the F2, but it was also meant to be another transitional model. In the summer of 1942, many Panzer IV G tanks were upgraded to the H variant, which became the main German late-war Panzer IV."
