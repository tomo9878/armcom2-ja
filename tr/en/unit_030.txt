L3/35 (HUN)	"The Kingdom of Hungary imported several hundred L3/35 tankettes from Italy before World War Two. A significant number of them were deployed in Yugoslavia and on the Eastern Front in 1941. The Hungarian variant was refitted with a more reliable 8mm machine gun."
38M Toldi I/II	"The Toldi light tank entered Hungarian service in 1940 and was first deployed in Yugoslavia. It was a fast tank equipped with a commander vision cupola, but its first two variants were only armed with a Solothurn anti-tank rifle and an 8mm machine gun. It stood no chance against advanced Soviet tanks such as the T-34."
38M Toldi IIa	"This improved variant of the Toldi light tank was introduced in 1942. Its main gun was upgraded to a calibre of 40mm and its armour was reinforced."
38M Toldi III	"Introduced in 1944, the Toldi III received another armour upgrade compared to the IIa variant. Only very few Toldi III tanks were produced before the end of World War Two."
43M Zrinyi II	"Named after the 17th century Hungarian leader Miklos Zrinyi, The 43M mounted a 105mm howitzer on the Turan chassis, and had improved frontal armour to offset its fixed turret. Only 66 units were ever produced."
39M Csaba	"Named after the legendary son of Attila the Hun, the Csaba remained the standard armoured car of the Royal Hungarian Army throughout the war despite its low production numbers. It was armed with a Solothurn anti-tank rifle and an 8mm machine gun."
40M Nimrod	"Based on a Swedish Landsverk L-62 design, the 40M Nimrod was equipped with a single Bofors AA gun with two gunners and two loaders to ensure a high rate of fire. Lightly armoured but mobile, The Nimrod was eventually provided with a powerful but short-ranged HEAT shell to be able to combat Soviet tanks. 135 were built."
38M Botond Truck	"First produced in 1938, the 38M Botond was a popular all-terrain truck used by the Royal Hungarian Army in World War Two."
Renault FT (BEL)	"First developed during the Great War, the Renault FT was the world's first modern tank. By the outbreak of the Second World War several thousand were still in use throughout the world. This model has a short-barreled 37mm main gun and is deployed in Belgian service."
ACG-1	"Designed in the 1930s, the AMC 35, or Automitrailleuse de Combat Renault modele 1935, was a French cavalry tank with limited production numbers. Rejected by the French military for doctrinal reasons, it was exported in very small numbers for the Belgian armoured forces."
Carden-Loyd Mk VI (47mm)	"Developed in 1927, The Carden-Loyd Mk VI was widely exported and was adapted into Polish TK-3 and TKS tankettes, and served as the basis for the British Universal Carrier, Italy's L3/35, and Japan's Type 94 Te-Ke. In the 1930s, Belgian engineers converted six vehicles into tank destroyers armed with 47mm anti-tank guns. They were cramped and overloaded, but they served as a test run for the more successful T13."
T13 B1	"The T13 B1 was a modified Vickers artillery tractor carrying a 47mm Model 1931 AT gun. It made up a large portion of Belgium's armoured forces in 1940."
T13 B3	"The T13 B3 was a modified Vickers artillery tractor carrying a 47mm Model 1931 AT gun. The B3 variant had a turret and modified the chassis to make it more suitable for combat."
Berliet VUDB	"The Berliet VUDB was a French 4x4 APC which went into production in 1929. They were not used widely by the French military with most sent to Africa. But some were exported to Belgium, converted later into recon vehicles. They were still in service by 1940."
Chevrolet 4x2 RD	"This design of truck was widespread during the 1930s and 1940s, produced by a number of different manufacturers around the world."

3-ton Truck	"This design of truck was widespread during the 1930s and 1940s, and was produced by a number of different manufacturers around the world."
Latil M2TL6 (FIN)	"Latil started to produce all-wheel-drive gun tractors for the French Army during the Great War, when around 2,000 Latils were in service by the end of the conflict. By 1940, this successor vehicle was also widely used by the French military, and some units were exported abroad to nations like Finland."
C2P	"The C2P artillery tractor was developed in Poland to tow the Bofors 40mm anti-aircraft gun, but they were sometimes also used to move heavier guns. More than 300 were delivered by the start of the war in 1939."
AS C2P(p)	"Many C2P artillery tractors were captured by the German forces during their 1939 invasion of Poland. They were designated Artillerie-Schlepper C2P(p), and were redeployed in subsequent invasions."
C4P	"The C4P artillery tractor was built upon the chassis of a Fiat 621 truck, and shared most parts except for the rear tracked drive. This variant was designed to tow artillery guns and for transporting ammo and supplies."
C4P (Heavy)	"The C4P artillery tractor was built upon the chassis of a Fiat 621 truck, and shared most parts except for the rear tracked drive. This variant was designed to tow heavier guns such as the 120mm Armata field gun."
C7P	"C7P was the heaviest Polish artillery tractor design, and it was the only Polish vehicle capable of towing 220mm super-heavy mortars. While over 300 were ordered, only half of that number was actually delivered by the start of the war in 1939."
AS C7P(p)	"Many C7P artillery tractors were captured by the German forces during their 1939 invasion of Poland. They were designated Artillerie-Schlepper C7P(p), and were redeployed in the subsequent invasions."
C7P (USSR)	"A few C7P artillery tractors were captured by Soviet forces during their invasion of eastern Poland and were redeployed into service during the early part of the war."
Unic P107	"Unic P107 was a French half-track prime mover, initially designed by Citroen but ultimately produced by Unic after Citroen went bankrupt and was re-acquired by Michelin. By June 1940, over 3000 were produced."
ZKW 37 U304(f)	"Unic P107 was a French half-track prime mover, initially designed by Citroen but ultimately produced by Unic after Citroen went bankrupt and was re-acquired by Michelin. By June 1940, over 3000 were produced. Most were captured by the Germans in good condition, and were redeployed as Zugkraftwagen 37 U304(f)."
Laffly S15T	"The Laffly S15T was designed to tow artillery pieces such as the 75mm field gun and the 105mm howitzer. Only around 400 were produced in total."
