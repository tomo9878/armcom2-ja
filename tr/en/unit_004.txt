Sturmmoerserwagen 606/4	"A vehicle born out of the experience of Battle of Stalingrad and the desperate need for heavy fire support in a brutal urban environment. The 380mm main gun launches rocket-assisted projectiles designed to destroy fortifications. Only 18 vehicles were ever produced, using rebuilt Tiger I chassis."
IS UE 630(f) Sfl. (early)	"Thousands of Renault UE were captured during the Battle of France and redeployed in German service. Around 700 of those were fitted with PaK 35/36 AT guns and deployed as makeshift tank destroyers, the majority on the Eastern Front. This early version simply fixed the gun onto the body with no further changes. "
IS UE 630(f) Sfl.	"Thousands of Renault UEs were captured during the Battle of France and redeployed in German service. Around 700 of those were fitted with PaK 35/36 AT guns and deployed as makeshift tank destroyers, the majority on the Eastern Front. This later version removes the lower part of the gun to save on weight. "
AS 630(r) Sfl.	"The T-20 Komsomolets was an armoured artillery tractor, developed in the mid-1930s alongside Soviet amphibitious tankettes, and armed with a single machine gun. Many were captured by the Germans during the early war, and were redeployed as Artillerie-Schlepper 630(r). Like with the captured Renault UE tractors, some were later equipped with PaK 36 anti-tank guns and served as makeshift tank destroyers."
Sd. Kfz. 10 GPZ (37mm)	"This version of the Sd. Kfz. 10 started to appear in 1942. In spite of its weak engine, it adds enough armour to protect the driver from shell fragments and rifle fire, even as the gun crew remains exposed. The gun itself is now mounted on its own pivot, allowing for superior traverse. Up to 900 of these conversions were produced."
Sd. Kfz. 10 GPZ (50mm)	"This was the ultimate tank destroyer conversion of Sd. Kfz. 10, since the Pak 38 was the strongest gun its chassis could bear. Appearing from 1942 onwards, it acted as a welcome substitute when Marders and medium tanks were not available, and was deployed as late as during the Normandy campaign."
Sd. Kfz. 6/3 GPZ	"This German half-track has had a captured Soviet F-22 76mm gun mounted in the rear, and was used as a makeshift tank destroyer. The first 9 vehicles were deployed in North Africa. Further production was cancelled after it became clear the vehicle was overloaded, very poorly armoured, and too easy to spot to be effective."
RSO (75mm)	"The Raupenschlepper Ost was developed in 1942 on the basis of tracked Soviet artillery tractors, in order to better deal with the Eastern Front conditions. 60 were fitted with armour plating and PaK 40 anti-tank guns and deployed in 1944. By then, this slow and highly vulnerable modification was no longer viable, and its nickname meant 'Rolling Coffin East'."
SPW 251/10	"This halftrack armoured transport has been fitted with a PaK 36 anti-tank gun."
SPW 251/22	"This is an SPW 251 halftrack transport fitted with a PaK 40 anti-tank gun, turning it into a dangerous tank destroyer."
Daimler DZVR 21	"This armoured car was designed for the Weimar Republic security forces. In 1945, the few surviving vehicles were taken out of storage and used to fight the Soviet troops on the streets of Berlin in spite of their obsolescence, with predictable results."
Sd. Kfz. 13	"The Kfz 13 was converted from an Adler touring car in 1935. Although lightly armoured, the vehicle is completely open on its top. These vehicles were deployed early during the war, but were pulled from front-line combat by the end of 1941."
PSW 221	"The Sd.Kfz.221 was designed in secret as an all-terrain armoured car, to replace the more limited Kfz.13. 339 vehicles were produced between 1935 and 1940. Although it is a mobile and capable scout, it lacks a radio and its off-road capabilities are fairly limited. Moreover, it was practically helpless against the other armoured vehicles, which led to a series of field modifications."
PSW 221 (late)	"After the Sd. Kfz. 221 encountered more armoured vehicles in France than anticipated, production ceased in favour of the Sd. Kfz. 222, and existing models were frequently upgunned with a Panzerbuchse 39 anti-tank rifle in addition to their main weapon. Some of these models were still in service in 1944."
PSW 221 (28mm)	"In 1941, some surviving PSW 221 armoured cars had their machine gun replaced with a heavy Mauser Panzerbuchse 41 anti-tank rifle. A total of 34 vehicles were converted in this manner."
PSW 222	"This autocannon-equipped light reconnaissance vehicle was much more capable than its predecessor, although due to a greater weight, it didn't handle as well. Over a thousand of these armoured cars were produced, most in the first three years. They performed well in Europe and North Africa, but were steadily displaced by half-tracks on the Eastern Front."
PSW 222 (late)	"By 1942, surviving PSW 222s were fitted with the upgraded KwK 38 2cm autocannon, and they received additional armour."
PSW 231 (6 rad)	"The Sd.Kfz. 231 was designed in the early 1930s around a civilian truck chassis. It proved excellent on dry ground but lacked true off-road capability and its production ended in 1937 after the 4-axle version was successfully introduced. The 140 units built by that point continued to be used for reconnaisance until 1942. "
PSW 231 (8 rad)	"The poor off-road performance of the 6-wheeled Sd.Kfz. 231 prompted the development of this model, which placed the engine at the rear and gave the driver better visibility. While less numerous than the 6-wheeled variant during the early war, its production picked up after the invasion of the USSR, with around 450 built between July 1941 and October 1943."
PSW 233	"This was a support variant of the PSW 231 (8 rad), armed with a fixed short-barreled 75mm gun."
PSW 234/1	"The Puma was a development of the earlier PSW 231 (8 rad), with improved armour and a more powerful engine. Its design specified a closed turret with same 5cm KwK L/60 gun as the Panzer III L, but those were in short supply, forcing the first 200 vehicles to use an open turret from a PSW 222 with a 20mm autocannon from the late Panzer II models."
PSW 234/2	"The Puma was a development of the earlier PSW 231 (8 rad) design, with improved armour and a more powerful engine."
PSW 234/3	"This was a successor to the Sd.Kfz.233 variant of PSW 231 (8 rad). It carried the same short 75mm gun to provide highly mobile fire support to infantry squads. Fewer than 100 were produced in late 1944 before it was displaced by the next variant."
SPW 250/9	"This halftrack has the turret from a PSW 222 or PSW 234/1 armoured car mounted on the back. Although not technically an Armoured Car, it was used in a similar role."
SPW 250/10	"This halftrack is armed with a 37mm gun and usually heads a platoon of SPW 250/9 vehicles as armoured reconnaissance. Although not technically an Armoured Car, it was used in a similar role."
Krupp Protze AA	"The Krupp Protze was extensively produced before the war as a tractor for light anti-tank guns and anti-aircraft guns. Some were modified with FlaK 30 anti-aircraft guns mounted onto their flatbeds, but half-track vehicles were soon considered preferable for this task."
Sd. Kfz. 10/4	"This was first self-propelled anti-aircraft gun in German service. The standard half-track prime mover was modified with fold-down side and rear panels and mounted a FlaK 30 AA gun. 370 were deployed in Poland, and over 1000 were ready for the Battle of France. In total, around 2000 were produced by March 1942, and they were never fully displaced by more advanced models."
Sd. Kfz. 10/5	"This successor to Sd. Kfz. 10/4 was widened in order to better fit a more advanced FlaK 38 AA gun, but was otherwise unchanged. Nearly 1000 were produced from late 1942 to 1945, though many were quickly uparmoured in the field, at the expense of their mobility."
Sd. Kfz. 10/5 GPZ	"Sd. Kfz. 10 light half-tracks were highly vulnerable even to shell fragments and rifle fire, and around a third of Sd. Kfz. 10/5 self-propelled AA guns were uparmoured in the field, with those variants known as 'Gepanzerte', or GPZ. Even this light plating had a deletirious impact on their mobility and engine performance."
Flakpanzer 38(t)	"The Flakpanzer 38(t) was a self-propelled anti-aircraft gun mounted on a Panzer 38(t) chassis. By the time this AA tank was introduced in April 1944, its single 2cm Flak 38 was no longer considered effective enough to shoot down Allied planes. Because of this, only a small number was built."
