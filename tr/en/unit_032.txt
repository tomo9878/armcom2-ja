SPA Dovunque 35 (GER)	"Dovunque means 'anywhere', as this light Italian truck was specifically designed to be able to travel off-road in North Africa, and was also widely deployed on the Eastern Front. Around 300 were produced under the German occupation."
Tatra 111	"This heavy truck was produced by the Czechoslovakian manufacturer Tatra and adopted by the Wehrmacht in 1943. Relatively few were produced  during the war, but it was extensively manufactured in the postwar period. Its Austrian designer was tried for collaboration and imprisoned for five years."
Citroen 23 (GER)	"6000 Citroen trucks were captured by the Germans after their victory in France, and around 4000 more were produced during occupation to serve German needs. Their reliability, however, was low because the workers had frequently sabotaged production with hard-to-spot 'errors'. A common approach was to place the wrong markings on the engine oil dipsticks."
Peugeot DMA	"This 2-ton truck was an evolution of the prewar DK5J. The majority of the vehicles produced were used by the Wehrmacht, as with other French trucks of the Vichy period."
Renault AHN	"After Paris fell, Louis Renault collaborated with the Germans, and his factories produced tens of thousands of trucks for the Wehrmacht. This truck was an improvement of the prewar Renault AGR. First deployed in Operation Barbarossa, it was withdrawn by 1942 due to poor performance in cold weather. Hundreds were delivered to the Afrika Korps and was widely used by forces occupying France and Italy."
Renault AHR	"This 5-ton model improved upon the earlier Renault AGK. It was first deployed during Operation Barbarossa, but was withdrawn from the USSR by 1942 due to poor performance in cold weather. Hundreds were delivered to the Afrika Korps was widely used by forces occupying Western Europe."
Laffly V15T	"The Laffly V15T was designed to tow light anti-tank guns for French cavalry divisions. For a lower profile, gun crew seats were placed directly on the floor, and its nickname refers to its 'butt-kicking' ride quality. No more than 200 were produced by the time of the German invasion, and even fewer were ultimately deployed before the French surrender."
Latil M2TL6	"Latil started to produce all-wheel-drive gun tractors for the French Army during the Great War, when around 2,000 Latils were in service by the end of the conflict. By 1940, this successor vehicle was also widely used by the French military, and it was exported abroad as well."
Somua MCG	"This half-track was one of the most modern and most capable gun tractors available to the French military during the Battle of France. 345 were produced, and were used to tow heavy artillery and evacuate damaged tanks."
Laffly S35T	"This heavy wheeled truck was designed to tow the heaviest French guns, such as 155mm GPF guns and the 220mm howitzers. Relatively few were produced."
Ford F15 (GER)	"The Ford F15 was one of the so-called Military Pattern trucks produced in Canada to outfit British and Commonwealth forces throughout the war. Many of these trucks were captured and redeployed by the German troops in North Africa."
Fiat 621 (USSR)	"Produced under license from the Italian Fiat company, the 621 serves as the workhorse of the Polish military. In September 1939, some of these trucks were captured by the Soviet Union in good condition and redeployed."
Citroen-Kegresse P19	"This French half-tracked vehicle was developed in 1932 to transport small teams for French cavalry regiments. Around 500 were produced before the decision was made to improve wheeled vehicles instead, and they were relegated to second-line forces."
Peugeot DK5J	"Unlike other French car manufacturers, Peugeot had little experience in producing vehicles with sufficient capacity and robustness to suit military requirements. Nevertheless, this light truck was rapidly pushed into production in the months between the invasion of Poland and the invasion of France."
Renault AGR	"This medium truck was one of the core designs used by the French army, with several thousand produced prior to the Battle of France."
Renault AGK	"This 5-ton truck was the main heavy transport vehicle used by the French army."
Fiat 626 (FRA)	"Prewar French diplomacy had a great investment in deepening ties with Italy, in the hope that they would support France in any conflict with Germany. One of the few fruits of this initiative was the delivery of 700 Italian trucks to the French military."
Humber Super Snipe	"This car was designed in 1938 for the civilian market, but was soon produced exclusively for the military. It was known for its reliability, with one car used by Montgomery in Normandy restored to running condition 24 hours after falling into Mulberry Harbour."
Citroen 23 (FIN)	"Originally designed for civilian service, these French Citroen trucks were widely exported, and they make up a large part of the Finnish military truck fleet."
Scania Vabis 335 (FIN)	"This heavy Swedish truck entered production in the mid-1930s, and it was widely exported to Finland. It was one of the few vehicles with a Hesselman engine, which was unlike either petrol or diesel engines and in some ways superior to both. All its advantages, however, were offset by the copious clouds of toxic smoke it generated."
Fiat 508 Coloniale	"Fiat 508 was a highly popular Italian passenger car of the 1930s. Its production run included this off-road capable version designed as a staff car for the Italian army."
Bianchi VM 6C	"The Bianchi VM 6C was developed from the ground up as a military staff car. While it lacked full-wheel drive, a powerful engine and a pair of wheels mounted in neutral above the road level provided it with great all-terrain mobility."
Lancia Aprilia Coloniale	"This staff car entered production in 1941, and around 250 were produced prior to Italy's surrender. While less off-road capable than the Bianchi VM, it had a remarkably luxurious interior for a military vehicle and was popular amongst the highest ranks."
Fiat 634N	"The Fiat 634N was the heaviest truck used by Italy during the war, and one of the few with a diesel engine. It was also the first truck in the world to feature foldable beds for its crew."
Isotta Fraschini D80 NM	"Named after its co-founders, Isotta Fraschini was born in 1900s as a Renault repair shop, and became a high-end company whose luxury cars were popular in prewar Hollywood. It also, however, manufactured these high-capacity diesel trucks, with NM being the military version."
Alfa Romeo 800	"This medium truck entered production in 1940 and it was one of the supply vehicles used by the Italian armed forces during World War 2. It was also exported to Hungary."
Lancia 3Ro	"This heavy truck was one of the supply vehicles used by the Italian armed forces during World War 2. Its truckbed was also used to mount artillery pieces."
SPA 38R	"This 2.5-ton truck was specifically designed for the Regio Esercito in the early 1930s, and over 16,000 were produced by the time of the 1943 armistice. While less advanced than the more modern trucks designs also used by Italy, it remained a reliable workhorse throughout the war."

SPA AS.37	"This light truck was built on the chassis of the TL 37 gun tractor, and was used exclusively to transport small teams off-road. At their peak, 800 were in service in North Africa."
