GSW 39H(f) (leFH 16)	"After taking over France, the Germans ended up with a surplus of H39 tanks in good working condition but of limited combat utility, so many had their turrets removed and were used to provide increased mobility to heavier field artillery like the WW1-era leFH 16 howitzers. They were never deployed outside of France, but were used extensively in 1944 until they were lost in the Falaise Pocket. "

GSW 39H(f) (75mm)	"Poor performance of early-war Wehrmacht tank guns against the Matilda, KV, and T-34 tanks drove a rush to install PaK 40 anti-tank guns on the hulls of any otherwise obsolete tracked vehicle possessed by the Germans. This included 24 captured H39 tanks, which were retained in France to bolster its defences, and fought against the Allies after the Normandy landings."
GSW FCM(f) (75mm)	"Nearly half of all FCM 36 tanks produced in France ended up captured by the Germans in good condition. Too slow and undergunned for the frontline, they provided an acceptable gun platform. Ten tanks had their turret removed and a PaK 40 anti-tank gun installed in a distinctive hull superstructure. Due to their diesel engine they neverleft France, but were deployed to fight the Allies after the Normandy landings."
Marder I	"By 1942, it was clear that heavy tanks would only become more numerous on the battlefield, yet few of the Wehrmacht's vehicles could counter them at range. One attempted solution involved mounting a PaK 40 onto a French Lorraine 37L gun tractor, called the Sd. Kfz. 135 (75mm). This increased its weight by a third, overloading the once-robust suspension and making it a poor fit for the Eastern Front."
Panzerjager 35R	"The Panzerjager I had given the obsolete Panzer I new utility as a tank destroyer, and it was eventually decided to fit 200 captured Renault R35 tanks with the same 4.7cm Czech anti-tank gun. This placed even more strain on the already underpowered drivetrain, and all Panzerjager 35Rs deployed in the USSR broke down within days. It was withdrawn by 1942 and only redeployed after the Normandy landings."
Panzerjager 35R 731(f)	"This was the command version of Panzerjager 35R, a tank destroyer based on the captured Renault R35 chassis. Its own gun had been replaced with an MG 34 in order to leave more space for the additional radio equipment. 26 of these vehicles were produced and provided to Panzerjager 35R squad leaders. It still suffered from the same reliability issues as the original design. "
AAC-1937 612(f)	"The AAC-1937 was a Spanish Republican attempt to imitate the Soviet BA-6 armoured car. Some of the crews escaped over the French border at the end of the war, and their vehicles were later used during the Battle of France. Around 30 vehicles were then captured and used by the Wehrmacht during Operation Barbarossa."
PSW P204(f)	"Panzerspahwagen P204 was the German designation for captured French Panhard P-178 armoured cars, also known as the AMD 35. Around 190 were redeployed during Operation Barbarossa, over half of which were lost by the end of 1941."
PSW P204(f) (late)	"After over half of the nearly 200 vehicles deployed during Operation Barbarossa were lost by the end of 1941, the remaining PSW P204s often received applique armour, increasing their protection at the expense of additional weight."
PSW AB41 201(i)	"After Italy signed an armistice with the Allies, fifty-seven Autoblindo 41 armoured cars were seized by the Germans and twenty-three were manufactured during the occupation. All were used in combat under this designation."
PSW AB43 203(i)	"At the time of the armistice two redesigns of the AB 41 were planned, one with a lower profile and one with a 47mm gun. The Germans were forced to combine them after taking over production. The AB 43 carried a smoke mortar and was more reliable, but only had a one-man turret."
PSW DAF 201(h)	"The M39 Pantserwagen was a Dutch armoured car produced in 1939. Only around a dozen of these vehicles were built before the German invasion. Nearly all were captured and redeployed in France and on the Leningrad Front."
Panzerspahwagen P204	"Panzerspähwagen P204 was the German designation for redeployed captured French Panhard P-178 armoured cars, also known as the AMD 35."
U304(f)	"The Unic P107 was a French half-track prime mover, initially designed by Citroen but produced by Unic after Citroen went bankrupt and was re-acquired by Michelin. By June 1940 over 3000 were produced. Most were captured by the Germans in good condition and were used as gun tractors. Some vehicles stationed in France were fitted with armour and machine gun mounts and served as armoured personnel carriers."
U304(f) (37mm)	"This captured Unic P107 has been fitted with armour and a Pak 36 anti-tank gun to turn it into a makeshift tank destroyer."
T-26E	"The Vickers 6-ton tank was a direct predecessor to the Soviet T-26 tank, and after the Winter War, it was decided to upgrade all remaining Finnish Vickers tanks to match the T-26C model, as well as redesigning them to carry a hull machine gun in place of the rear turret one. The OT-133 tanks captured in 1941 were also modified in the same manner."
AB 41 (ROM)	"Eight of these Italian armoured cars were exported to Romania in 1943. They were used as a scouting force in the 1st Armoured Division. "
T-38 (ROM)	"After many of the original vehicles of the 2nd Tank Regiment were lost on the Eastern Front, it was decided to replenish the unit with a delivery of 50 Pz. 38s in May of 1943."
T-38 (HUN)	"After most of Hungarian armour was lost in 1941, over 100 Pz. 38s were transferred to replenish their capabilities. Yet by 1942 they were of very limited utility against Soviet armour."
LT-35 (USSR)	"The LT-35 was intended to equip the Czechoslovakian army, but over 200 vehicles were seized in March 1939 and it entered service as the Panzerkampfwagen 35(t). It was a balanced design for the early war, but far too unreliable next to 38(t), and no new tanks or spare parts were produced by 1940. These tanks were the first confirmed trophies taken by the Soviets during Operation Barbarossa."
R-1	"R-1 is the Romanian designation for Czechoslovakia's AH-IV tankette. A few of these were captured and redeployed by the defenders of Odessa in 1941."
R-35 VdC	"The poor anti-armour performance of R-35's 37mm gun prompted the development of Vanatorul de Care (Tank Hunter), which mounted a 45mm gun recovered from T-26s, BA-10s, BT-5s and BT-7s. Intended to fight Soviet tanks, R-35 VdC was not ready until Romania surrendered to the USSR, and 30 units ended up serving alongside them. "
TACAM T60	"A Romanian tank destroyer based on a captured T-60 tank. The turret was removed and replaced with a captured Soviet 7.6cm M-1939 F-22 field gun, and a BMG was installed. Several dozen were ready by 1944 and deployed against the Soviet forces, but they were unable to make a significant difference. A few were later deployed against the Germans and the Hungarians."
R-2 TACAM	"A tank destroyer conversion of the R-2, which replaced the turret with with a captured Soviet F-22 field gun. Only 20 were ever produced, but it had proved itself useful in battles against Hungarian vehicles after Romania had switched sides."
LT-38 Praga	"This is a Panzer 38(t) E which has been captured by the Red Army, and redeployed in Soviet service."
41M Turan II (USSR)	"Small numbers of Hungarian medium Turan tanks were captured and redeployed by the Soviet Union during battles in Hungary."
P204 (USSR)	"Panzerspahwagen P204 was the German designation for captured French Panhard P-178 armoured cars, also known as the AMD 35. Around 190 were deployed during the invasion of the USSR, and over half were lost by the end of 1941. Some of those lost vehicles were repurposed by the Soviet defenders, becoming 'double trophies'."
FTR Carrier Mk. IIC (M)	"This Canadian variant of the Universal Carrier is armed with a flamethrower fed from a single fuel tank in the front and a 2-inch mortar in the rear compartment. While less widely produced than the other variants, it still played an important role in infantry support. Eventually, every battalion had six Wasps of either variant attached to it."
AMR 33	"The AMR 33 was designed as a cavalry tank, intended for light reconnaissance."
