Valentine X	"The Valentine Mk X was similar to the Mk IX variant, but it received a redesigned turret which allowed to reintroduce the co-axial Besa machine gun. The turret still only had room for two crewmen. It was only used as a command tank and usually not deployed in direct combat."
Valentine XI	"This rare late variant of the Valentine was the only one still deployed on the frontlines in the last two years of the war. It was armed with a 75mm gun and restricted to unit commanders."
FTR Carrier Mk. II	"This variant of the Universal Carrier is armed with a single flamethrower and carries two large fuel tanks. It was less widely used than the improved Canadian version, but several hundred were still deployed in Normandy."
Sherman IC	"The 'Firefly' series of tanks replaced the standard 75mm main gun with a larger 17pdr, with much better penetration power. This made the tank slower and heavier, and some ammo had to be stored where the assistant driver was once seated. Most conversions were performed on the latest Sherman V models, but the demand for a heavy tank killer was such that some Sherman Is were converted before the Normandy landings."
Sherman IC Hybrid	"The Sherman IC Hybrid 'Firefly' was a standard M4A1 tank converted to carry a 17pdr main gun, with far superior armour penetration power compared to standard 75mm guns. Because of the large size of the main gun ammo, the assistant driver position and its BMG has been removed. These conversions used an M4 composite hull, with a cast front end and a welded rear hull."
Sherman VC	"The Sherman VC 'Firefly' was a standard M4A4 tank converted to carry a 17pdr main gun, with far superior armour penetration power compared to standard 75mm guns. Because of the large size of the main gun ammo, the assistant driver position and its BMG has been removed."
Cromwell I	"The Cromwell tank was faster and smaller than the M4 Sherman, but it did not benefit from sloped armour. This early variant still relied on the OQF 6-pounder as its main armament. It was only produced in small numbers."
Cromwell IV	"The Cromwell tank was faster and smaller than the M4 Sherman, but it did not benefit from sloped armour. The Mk IV was its most common variant, armed with a 75mm gun."
Cromwell VI	"This close support variant of the Cromwell was armed with an OQF 95mm howitzer. It was only produced in small numbers."
Challenger	"Derived from the Cromwell, the Challenger was armed with the powerful OQF 17-pounder. However, its turret armour and mobility had to be reduced to compensate for the increased weight of the main gun."
Comet	"Developed from the Cromwell tank, the Comet was equipped with a modified version of the OQF 17-Pounder gun that used shorter ammo. The Comet was slower but more reliable than the Cromwell and received additional armour protection. It only saw action during the final months of the Allied advance into Germany."
Churchill I	"The Churchill Mk I was armed with an OQF 2-pounder and a hull-mounted 3-inch howitzer. It saw action in the Dieppe Raid, in Tunisia, and Italy, but only about 300 tanks of this Churchill variant were ever built."
Churchill II	"The Churchill Mk II was a revision of the Mk I, sometimes called the Mk Ia, that replaced the hull-mounted howitzer with a 7.92mm Besa machine gun. It saw action in the Dieppe Raid, in Tunisia, and Italy. About 1,100 tanks of this Churchill variant were built in 1941 and 1942."
Churchill II Special	"Churchill Mk II 'Oke' was a special modification that added a hull-mounted flamethrower located to the left of the Assistant Driver's position. Three such tanks were part of the Dieppe raid in August 1942."
Churchill III	"The Mark III mounted a 6-pdr gun as its main armament."
Churchill IV	"The Mark IV is virtually identical to the earlier Mark III, the only difference being that the Mark IV has a cast turret."
Churchill IV NA(75)	"In Tunisia, some Churchill Mark IIIs and IVs had their original OQF 6-pounder gun replaced with an American 75mm gun from a destroyed Sherman or the British QF 75mm gun. This gave them a much better HE round. As many as 200 of this North African (NA) variant were created and served in Italy."
Churchill V	"This Churchill variant is armed with an OQF 95mm howitzer for close infantry support."
Churchill VI	"This variant was equipped with a 75mm gun, more effective than the 6pdr mounted on earlier models. Churchill Mark IVs that were upgraded with this gun were functionally the same, and known as Churchill IV NA(75)."
Churchill VII	"The Churchill Mk VII received a significant armour upgrade compared to earlier variants and was equipped with an OQF 75mm gun."
Churchill VIII	"This is the close support variant of the heavily armoured Churchill Mk VII, equipped with a 95mm howitzer."
Churchill AVRE	"This is the Armoured Vehicle Royal Engineers (AVRE) variant of the Churchill Mk IV. Instead of the main turret gun, it is armed with a Petard heavy mortar that can only fire at close-range targets."
Churchill Crocodile	"The Churchill Crocodile was a conversion kit for MK VII Churchills which replaced the hull MG with a flamethrower. 500 of these vehicles were produced and used between 1944 and 1945."

Bishop	"The Bishop was a self-propelled OQF 25-pounder howitzer. Introduced in late 1942, it was used in North Africa and during the early parts of the Italian campaign. It was a slow, unreliable vehicle which suffered from a lack of gun elevation. Because of this, the Bishop was only produced in small numbers."
