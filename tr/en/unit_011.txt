M4 HST	"The M4 High Speed Tractor was an artillery tractor that used the tracks from a Sherman tank. It normally towed either the 90mm anti-aircraft gun or the 155mm howitzer. It was faster than its foreign equivalents, and was also equipped with an M2 Browning machine gun for self-defense. Nearly 6000 were produced between 1943 and 1945."
M5 HST	"The M5 High Speed Tractor was an artillery tractor designed and produced in parallel to the M4. Unlike the M4, it used the tracks and the suspension from the Stuart tank, yet it was similar in all other respects. It entered production two months after the M4 HST, and around 5,200 were produced by the end of the war."
M5 HST (USSR)	"The M5 High Speed Tractor was an artillery tractor designed and produced in parallel to the M4. Unlike the M4, it used the tracks and the suspension from the Stuart tank, yet it was similar in all other respects. It entered production in May 1943, and 200 units were delivered to the Red Army in 1944."
M6 HST	"Contrary to its name, the M6 High Speed Tractor was a lot slower than the M4 and M5 HST, but this was more than offset by its ability to tow the super-heavy guns like the 240mm 'Black Dragon' howitzer. Around 1000 were produced in 1944 and 1945, and they were deployed exclusively on the European theater."
M33 Prime Mover	"The M33 Prime Mover was an artillery tractor converted from the M3 Lee chassis, designed to tow the heaviest guns in the US service. 109 vehicles were converted in 1943-1944, and they were deployed in Italy and in Normandy."
M34 Prime Mover	"The M34 Prime Mover was an artillery tractor converted from M4A1 Sherman chassis. 24 such conversions were performed in 1944."
M35 Prime Mover	"The M35 Prime Mover was an artillery tractor converted from M10 Wolverine tank destroyer in order to tow the heaviest artillery pieces in the US Army. Over 200 conversions were performed before the introduction of the M6 HST."
Mack No 6x6 HAR	"The Mack Heavy Artillery Tractor was a 6-wheeled, all-wheel drive truck capable of towing the heaviest guns in the US Army. Around 2000 were produced starting from June 1943."

The 'Kangaroo', however, was neither well-protected nor easy to get in and out of, and so they were typically used as armoured gun tractors instead."
Stuart Kangaroo (late)	"Even the M5 version of the Stuart was largely outclassed by both newer German designs and other allied vehicles by the time it was introduced. Like with the M3 variant, the British modified some vehicles into 'Kangaroos' by removing the turret. Though initially intended to serve as an improvised APC, they were typically used as armoured gun tractors instead."
Willys MB (USSR)	"The famous Willys MB Jeep was one of the most popular off-road utility vehicles among the US forces and their allies in World War 2. Lend-Lease deliveries to the USSR began in the summer of 1942."
Ford 4	"The Ford GPA was the amphibious conversion of the Willys MB Jeep. It performed very poorly when used for naval landings by the American troops, but it proved far more capable at crossing rivers and lakes in Eastern Europe, and was in high demand in the USSR, where it was given this designation."
GMC DUKW (USSR)	"The DUKW, also known as LVW, was an amphibious variant of the widespread General Motors 2.5 ton truck. It was the first military vehicle where tire pressure could be regulated from inside the cab, and was capable of crossing the English Channel unassisted. Over 20,000 were produced, and 586 were provided to the USSR through Lend-Lease."
Studebaker US6	"The Studebaker Corporation lost their bid to supply the US military with trucks to GMC, and switched to export production to satisfy the needs of the Allies. Around 150,000 of these trucks were sent to the USSR, with many of the surviving machines returned after the war under the terms of the Lend-Lease agreements. Their all-wheel drive and high ground clearance resulted in an excellent off-road performance."
