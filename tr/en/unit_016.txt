Type 73 Carden-Loyd Mk VI	"Developed in 1927, The Carden-Loyd Mk VI was the cheapest and most widely exported tank in the world. It was directly adapted into Polish TK-3 and TKS tankettes, and served as the basis for the British Universal Carrier, Italy's L3/35, and Japan's Type 94 Te-Ke. 10 units were used by Thailand versus France in 1941."
Type 77 Carden-Loyd Mk VI	"This is the 1930 version of the Carden Loyd tankette. The only changes done to the 1927 design made it slightly more reliable and even cheaper to produce and it now costed one-third of the similarly widely exported Renault FT. The Royal Thai Army possessed 30 Type 77s by 1940."
Type 76 VCL	"These Vickers-Carden-Loyd tankettes became the basis for the Soviet T-37A. Only a handful were ever exported to Thailand, where their ability to cross water independently helped to offset their slow speed and poor reliability."
Type 76 SPAAG	"This British design mounted the Swedish Bofors 40mm AA gun atop Vickers 6-ton chassis. Its Thai designation refers to its adoption year of 1933 - 2476 under the Buddhist calendar. 26 units were imported, and ended up instrumental in suppressing the 1935 Boworadet Rebellion."

Type 76 Vickers 6-Ton	"First built in 1928, the Vickers Mark E was designed in Britain but all production models were licensed and built abroad. The Type A model with twin machine gun turrets was known as Type 76 in Thailand. "
Type 81 Vickers 6-Ton	"A dozen Type B models of the Vickers Mark E were imported to Thailand in 1938, or year 2481 under the local calendar. While it was also obsolete, it was still far more suitable to the conditions of Indochina than the colonial Renault FTs."
AEC Matador	"The Matador was a powerful 4x4 truck designed by the Associated Equipment Company in order to tow the heavier guns in the British Army. It was a successful design and around 9,000 vehicles were made, including an extended post-war usage for civilian purposes. Its production run did not end until 1953."
Scammell Pioneer R100	"The Scammell Pioneer was first designed in 1927, but it was not adapted for military purposes until 1937. It was the most powerful British military tractor, capable of pulling super-heavy artillery or evacuating any tank."

Loyd Carrier TT	"Over 25,000 Loyd Carriers were produced from 1939 to 1944. By the time of the Normandy landings, it was primarily used to tow 6-pdr guns in its Tracked Towing (TT) configuration."
