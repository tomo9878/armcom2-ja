Panzer IV J	"Introduced in February 1944, this was the last Panzer IV variant to be produced. The J variant was designed to facilitate production compared to the H variant. Sturmpanzer I	"The Poland campaign had shown that the heavy, short-range 15cm sIG 33 guns had trouble keeping up with the Blitzkrieg, so some were mounted onto a Panzer I chassis. This design was, however, overloaded and left little space for ammo, so all production was cancelled after the Battle of France. Most of the remaining vehicles were lost during Operation Barbarossa, and the survivors were transferred to the Caucasus front in 1942."
Sturmpanzer II	"The Sturmpanzer II was an attempt to improve on its heavily flawed predecessor by mounting the same heavy gun onto a Panzer II chassis. It didn't provide much of an improvement and was still a unreliable, overloaded vehicle with a low ammo capacity, so only 12 vehicles were ever built. All of them were deployed in North Africa in 1942, and all were lost to breakdowns and enemy fire by the end of that year."
Sturmpanzer IV	"The Sturmpanzer IV, nicknamed 'Brummbaer' (Grizzly Bear), was a rare howitzer variant of the Panzer IV, intended to provide artillery support from a safe distance. The howitzer superstructure was heavily armoured."
Sturmpanzer IV (late)	"This is a late-production model of Sturmpanzer IV. It has a lighter gun mount and a redesigned casemate to lessen strain on the front drivetrain.
