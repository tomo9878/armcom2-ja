Diamond T G509	"This was a heavy, full-wheel drive truck developed for the US Army,  designed to carry a load of 4 tons. Around 30,000 were produced throughout the war, including a range of specialized vehicles."
GMC DUKW 353	"The DUKW, also known as LVW, was an amphibious variant of the widespread General Motors 2.5 ton truck. It was the first military vehicle where tire pressure could be regulated from inside the cab, and it was capable of crossing the English Channel unassisted. Over 20,000 were produced, and they were in service from between the Normandy Landings until the end of the war."

FGS Bren(e) (25mm)	"A large number of 25mm anti-tank guns were captured after the Battle of France. They were considered too weak for the Eastern Front or for defending captured European countries from an Allied counterattack, but not for the North African theater. Unlike the trophy Renault UEs, the Universal Carriers captured at Dunkirk struggled to fit a PaK 36, so they were equipped with these weapons and provided to Afrika Korps."
FGS Bren(e) (47mm)	"The Universal Carrier was developed in 1934 on the basis of a Carden Loyd tankette. A considerable number was captured by the Germans at Dunkirk, and they experimented with fitting a range of anti-tank weapons to them like a PaK 36 or a captured 2-pdr gun. 47mm Austrian Bohler gun performed the best, and some 'Fahrgestell' carriers equipped with these guns were deployed in Normandy."
M-H II (GER)	"The Marmon-Herrington was a South African-designed armoured car, intended for domestic defense. When war broke out in North Africa, this model was the only Commonwealth-produced Armoured Car available in significant numbers. Some were captured and reused by the Germans, and were commonly modified with a 28mm heavy anti-tank rifle."

Flakpanzer Bren 731(e)	"Over 50,000 Universal Carriers were produced between 1934 and 1945, most of the Bren Gun Carrier modification. From the Battle of France onwards, many fell into the German hands, and they were often adapted for other purposes. Panzerjager Bren 731(e)	"The Universal Carrier was developed in 1934 and over 50,000 were produced by 1945. At that point, they were reassigned to transport Panzerschreck teams."
Mark VII	"The MK VII Tetrarch was a British tank designed to be light enough to be air-droppable. It entered production after the Battle of France, but it was not deployed until D-Day due to its inability to handle North Africa's climate. While it is very light, its tracks are very narrow, limiting off-road mobility. 20 Tetrarchs were provided to the USSR, and were deployed in the Caucasus."
