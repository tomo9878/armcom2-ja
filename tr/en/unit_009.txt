M4A2 Sherman	"This was a diesel-powered variant of the Sherman tank with a General Motors twin G-41 engine. These were used by allied forces as well as the American Marine corps."
M4A3 Sherman	"The M4A3 has a welded hull and carries a powerful Ford GAA V8 engine. It ended up being the most commonly used model in the American forces. This version has a vision cupola for the commander."
M4A3R5 Sherman	"These M4A3 Sherman tanks were modified by Navy engineers to replace the main gun with a flamethrower. Known as Zippo or Ronson after popular brands of lighters, Marines called them the Mark 1. Only a small number saw action on Iwo Jima, but they were highly effective."
M4A3(75)W Sherman	"This is a later variant of the M4A3 with glacis front hull armour and wet stowage for main gun rounds, reducing the chance of fire from a ruptured shell casing. Some are built with Horizontal Volute Spring Suspension, offering better traction. A Smoke Mortar is mounted on the turret, fired by the Loader."
M4A3E2(75)W Sherman	"Designed to fulfill the role of a heavily armoured assault tank, this is an up-armoured version of the M4A3 with a new turret. A Smoke Mortar is mounted on the turret, fired by the Loader."
M4A3E2(76)W Sherman	"Designed to fulfill the role of a heavily armoured assault tank, this is an up-armoured version of the M4A3 with a new turret. This is a conversion of the standard 75mm gun to a 76mm gun. A Smoke Mortar is mounted on the turret, fired by the Loader."
M4A1(76)W Sherman	"By 1942 it was clear that the 75mm gun on the Sherman was not sufficient to penetrate newer, heavy tanks. By 1944 some models of M4A1 were fitted with the 76mm gun, and these were rushed to France  soon after the Normandy landings."
M4A3(76)W Sherman	"This is a later variant of the M4A3, with a 76mm main gun and wet stowage for main gun rounds. The variants with HVSS were designated E8 and popularly known as 'Easy Eights'."
M4(105) Sherman	"First designed in 1940, the M4 'Sherman' tank entered production in 1942 and became the main battle tank for Allied forces in the war. This late-war variant was equipped with a 105mm howitzer for infantry support."
M4A3(105) Sherman	"The M4A3 has a welded hull and carries a powerful Ford GAA V8 engine. This rare variant was equipped with a 105mm howitzer for infantry support."
M26 Pershing	"The M26 Pershing was the only heavy American tank deployed in the war. In its few engagements, its 90mm main gun turned out to be a formidable weapon even against heavy German armour."
M3 GMC	"The M3 Gun Motor Carriage, an early American tank destroyer, was a lightly armoured half-track equipped with an M1897A5 75mm gun. It served in the Philippines campaign, the Pacific, North Africa, and in the Allied invasion of Sicily, but was eventually replaced by the M10 Wolverine."
M6 GMC Fargo	"The M6 Gun Motor Carriage 'Fargo' was the first US tank destroyer of the war. It had a 37mm gun mounted on a Dodge WC 52 3/4 ton truck. While it was initially considered a cost-effective mobile anti-tank weapon, it soon fell into disfavor in North Africa because its 37mm gun turned out to be ineffective against German tanks of the time."
M10 Wolverine	"Introduced in early 1943, the M10 GMC, nicknamed Wolverine, proved itself as an effective tank destroyer against German targets. Its open top, however, made it vulnerable to close-range infantry attacks."
M18 Hellcat	"The M18 GMC 'Hellcat' was designed to be light and fast, and used the light 76mm gun from the Sherman. Such speed comes at a cost, however, and it only has minimal armour protection."
M36 Jackson	"With its powerful main gun, the 90mm GMC M36 Jackson was the most effective American WW2 tank destroyer. It was significantly slower than the Hellcat, but its reinforced front armour offered far better protection."
M7 Priest	"The M7 Priest was designed as a self-propelled 105mm howitzer, using the chassis of an M3 Lee. It was first produced in April 1942. It received the nickname 'Priest' because its machine gun ring looked like a pulpit."
M7 Priest (UK)	"The M7 Priest was designed as a self-propelled 105mm howitzer, using the chassis of an M3 Lee. It was first produced in April 1942. M8 HMC	"Based on the M5 Stuart, the 75mm Howitzer Motor Carriage, later nicknamed 'Scott,' was designed as an assault gun and to provide mobile artillery support. It was used by American and Free French forces in Europe, and by the Marines in the Pacific."
M12 GMC	"This assault gun mounted a licensed copy of the 1917 French 155mm gun on the chassis of an M3 Lee. Around 100 were produced in 1942, and all were deployed in Normandy. "
M40 GMC	"This assault gun was built upon the M12 Gun Motor Carriage, mounting the more modern 'Long Tom' gun onto a more reliable M4A3 tank chassis, which had also increased its ammo capacity. Only a few were available during the final months of World War 2, but they were deployed more widely later during the Korean War."

It was a common practice to store a Bazooka anti-tank weapon inside the car for emergencies."
