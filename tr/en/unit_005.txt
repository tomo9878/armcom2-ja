Flakpanzer IV Wirbelwind	"The Flakpanzer IV Wirbelwind was a self-propelled 2cm Flakvierling 38 anti-aircraft gun mounted on a Panzer IV chassis. Despite its low production number, it was a fairly successful AA tank."
Flakpanzer IV Mobelwagen	"The Flakpanzer IV Mobelwagen was a self-propelled 3.7cm Flak 43 anti-aircraft gun mounted on a Panzer IV chassis."
Sd. Kfz. 7/1	"This is a German half-track vehicle armed with a 2cm Flakvierling 38 anti-aircraft gun. It was the most common German self-propelled AA gun in World War 2."
Sd. Kfz. 7/2	"This German half-track carries a 37mm anti-aircraft gun. It entered service around the same time as the 7/1, and close to 700 were produced by the end of the war."
Flakpanzer I	"Following the Battle of France, FlaK 38 autocannons were mounted on 24 Panzer I A hulls, in an attempt to create a better SPAAG. As with the Panzerjager I, the chassis was too small for the heavier weapon, and its fixed traverse meant it was not effective in an anti-air role. Instead, all were used to provide anti-infantry support around Kharkov in 1942, and all were lost at Stalingrad."
SPW 250/1	"This halftrack is designed to transport mechanized infantry units."
SPW 250/7	"This halftrack is armed with an 80mm mortar for infantry support."
SPW 250/8	"This halftrack is armed with the same low-velocity gun as the early Panzer IV. It is intended to serve as infantry support."
SPW 251/1	"This halftrack was one of the first armoured personnel carriers in the world. Though open-top, it still offered good protection versus small arms fire and allowed mechanized infantry divisions to overwhelm the territory already struck by bombers and tanks before the defenders could regroup. Over 15,000 were produced throughout the war."
SPW 251/2	"This armoured halftrack carries an 81mm mortar and its crew, and is designed for close infantry support."
SPW 251/1 II Wurfrahmen	"Some SPW 251 armoured halftracks were fitted with six 280mm Wurfrahmen rocket launchers. This design was initially conceived in 1940, but did not reach the frontline until 1943. A single Wurfrahmen rocket had explosive power equivalent to a 500kg bomb, and the vehicle was nicknamed 'Stuka on Foot'."
IS UE 630(f) Wurfrahmen	"Thousands of Renault UEs were captured during the Battle of France and redeployed in German service. After 1943, over 50 vehicles were also used to carry four Wurfrahmen 280mm rocket launcher tubes."
Reihenwerfer	"The Reihenwerfer mounted two arrays of 8 81mm mortars onto the captured French  Somua MCG chassis. It had impressive firepower but a much shorter range compared to rocket artillery like the Wurfrahmen, Soviet Katushas, or the Allied Calliopes. Unlike them, it was also fired one-by-one, rather than as a simultaneous burst."
Sd. Kfz. 4	"SPW 250 and SPW 251 APCs provided a key advantage to the German troops, but their production could not keep up with demand. In an attempt to cover the shortfall, around 1500 Maultier halftracks, produced by combining a truck body with a Panzer II track set, were given a new armoured body in order to create this APC."
Panzerwerfer 42	"This halftrack mounts a rotatable multiple rocket launcher. Although intended for long-range artillery support, they sometimes find themselves on the front lines of fighting."
Kfz 1	"The Volkswagen Type 82 'Kubelwagen' was a popular command car and often transported German officers throughout the war. It had a great off-road performance thanks to high ground clearance and a completely flat underside which allowed it to essentially slide over rough terrain without getting bogged down."
Tempo Vidal G1200	"The Tempo Vidal G1200 was designed in 1937 as a light scout/command car. Its dual-engine, all-wheel drive construction with a spare set of wheels provided excellent off-road mobility, but it was also expensive and  few were made compared to the Kubelwagen. Many of these vehicles were exported to allies, and the rest were mostly sent to the Eastern Front."
Borgward B 3000	"This was one of the main trucks used by the Wehrmacht. Widespread during the early war, it was found inferior to the Opel Blitz, yet its production continued until the Bremen-Sebaldsbrudk plant was destroyed by a bombing raid in 1944. 30,000 units were built in total."
Einheitsdiesel 2500	"In the mid-1930s, the Wehrmacht aimed to field standardized, 6-wheeled, full-wheel drive military trucks which could be produced in multiple size classes. The Einheitsdiesel was costlier than other trucks, and Germany could not afford to both supply trucks with diesel and tanks with petrol, so production ended in 1940. Remaining units served on the Eastern Front to 1944."
Henschel 33 3000	"While this design dates back to 1928, thousands of these trucks were still produced between 1934 and 1942. It was deployed in Poland, France and in the Soviet Union before getting displaced by the more modern trucks. Their last deployment in a combat operation was in Italy in 1943."
Horch 108	"This was the larger version of the Horch 901 staff car, used primarily to transport small teams around the battlefield. It was also at times used to tow light anti-tank guns and anti-aircraft guns. In total, around 8000 were produced between 1937 and 1942."
Opel Blitz 3-ton	"The Blitz truck, produced by the General Motors subsidiary company Opel, provides the motorized transport backbone for the German army. The 3-ton variant is the most common variant in use. It is the fastest wartime truck in its payload category."
Buessing-NAG 4500 Truck	"This 4.5-ton truck is the workhorse of the German armed forces. It combines a reliable diesel engine with good performance on rough terrain."
Mercedes-Benz L3000	"This was one of the main truck models used by the Wehrmacht. Around 30,000 units were built. It was slower than the Opel Blitz, yet it was more reliable on rough terrain, and was favoured by the Afrika Korps."
Kfz. 15	"This was the Wehrmacht designation for Horch 901, which was the second most-widely produced German staff car after the Kubelwagen. While slower, it was much more comfortable and could carry more people and larger payloads. Nearly 20,000 were produced between 1936 and 1943, at which point its production was assessed as too resource-intensive for the role it played."
T-I	"The Panzer I was first produced in the early 1930s, and was intended to serve as a training vehicle until heavier tanks could be produced. It was badly outdated in 1941, and small numbers were recaptured and briefly used by the Soviet forces."
T-II	"Around 75% of the German tanks used at the start of Operation Barbarossa were lost by 1942. While less catastrophic than the Soviet loss rate, it still meant that many tanks ended up abandoned on the battlefield and available for redeployment."
Jagdpanzer 38 (ROM)	"The simplicity of this tank destroyer enabled its rapid production. 15 units were supposed to be provided to the Kingdom of Romania in September, but after the country switched sides after King Michael's Coup this delivery was cancelled. Nevertheless, two units were captured from the Germans, and were briefly put into service."
SPW usor	"This German halftrack is designed to transport mechanized infantry units. Around a dozen were purchased by Romania in 1943, along with similar numbers of the SPW 251. Usor means 'light' in Romanian, in contrast to the medium SPW 251."
SPW mijlociu	"This German halftrack armoured transport is designed to carry infantry from armoured divisions quickly over terrain. Around a dozen were purchased by Romania in 1943. Its name means 'medium', in contrast to the 'light' SPW 250, which was acquired as well."
