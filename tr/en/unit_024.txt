GAZ-M1	"This was the second mass-produced passenger car in the Soviet Union's history. It was based on Ford Model B blueprints, extensively modified in order to deal with the Soviet roads and to be easy to repair. The M stood for Molotov, Chairman of the Council of People's Commissars. Around 60,000 were produced between 1936 and 1942, with 15,000 entering the military as staff cars."
GAZ-61	"While the GAZ-M1 was designed to cope with poor roads, it was still at its core a passenger car, and was prone to bogging down in the mud. As such, this full-wheel drive modification was developed in 1941. While much more mobile than M1, only a few hundred were produced before the cheaper and more reliable GAZ-67 entered production."
GAZ-AA	"This light truck was based on the American Ford AA. Produced since 1932, it was the most widespread Red Army vehicle, with 150,000 available in 1941, and many more produced by 1945, although it was increasingly shifted to the rear as newer trucks became more widely available. Its nickname refers to both its 1.5 ton capacity and the old naval cry to 'Look out below!', traditionally used by sailors when rigging the masts."
GAZ-AAA	"This was the six-wheeled version of the GAZ-AA truck, with a correspondingly lower ground pressure and a superior off-road performance. Around 35,000 were produced before its factory was destroyed in a bombing raid at the end of 1943. It was also used as the basis for the heavier BA armoured cars."
ZIS-5	"This 3-ton truck was the second most widely used truck of the Red Army after the GAZ-AA. Its reliability led to a nickname 'Zakhar', which shortens 'Za Kharakter' (For Good Character) into a common first name."
ZIS-6	"This was a six-wheeled version of the 3-ton ZIS-5. It was superior to the GAZ-AAA, and over 20,000 were built by June 1941. After its factory was destroyed in October 1941, no more could be built in the simpler factories located further away from the frontline, and remaining units were almost entirely replaced by the Lend-Lease Studebaker US6."
ZIS-6 (half-track)	"The ZIS-6 chassis was used as the basis for BA-6 and BA-10 armoured cars. Consequently, it could also have 'Overall' half-track kits attached to its four rear wheels, which was done to improve its mobility during the winter."
YaG-6	"This heavy truck was produced by the Yaroslavl' Automotive Factory. It uses the same engine as the lighter ZIS-5, resulting in poor mobility. 8000 were produced up until 1944, when the factory was retooled to produce gun tractors. In 1941, a six-wheeled variant was used to carry a 29K AA gun."
GAZ-M1 (FIN)	"This was the second mass-produced passenger car in the Soviet Union's history. It was based on Ford Model B blueprints, extensively modified in order to deal with the Soviet roads and be easy to repair. The M stood for Molotov, Chairman of the Council of People's Commissars. Around 60,000 were produced between 1936 and 1942, with 15,000 entering the military as staff cars.Some were captured and reused by Finland."
GAZ-AA (FIN)	"This light truck was based on the American Ford AA. Produced since 1932, it was the most widespread Red Army vehicle, with 150,000 available in 1941. Some of these vehicles ended up captured and then redeployed by the Finnish military."
ZIS-5 (FIN)	"This 3-ton truck is the workhorse of the Soviet armed forces.Considerable numbers were captured during the Winter War and in the first year of the Continuation War."
Renault FT Kegresse (POL)	"The Renault FT was the world's first modern tank design emerging from the Great War. Its low speed, however, was an obvious shortcoming, and some units revised the design with a more powerful engine, and Kégresse tracks and suspension. By 1939, even those few modernized models were still hopelessly slow and unreliable."
Renault FT (POL)	"The Renault FT was the first turreted tank of the Great War, and it was widely produced afterwards. By the outbreak of the Second World War, several thousand were still in use throughout the world, including nearly 100 in Poland. This model is armed with a single short-barreled 37mm gun."
Renault FT (MG, POL)	"The Renault FT was the first turreted tank of the Great War, and it was widely produced afterwards. By the outbreak of the Second World War, several thousand were still in use throughout the world, including nearly 100 in Poland. This model is armed only with a turret-mounted machine gun."
TK-3	"Polish armoured forces in 1939 are dominated by tankettes, with around half being of the TK-3 variant. Originally developed in 1930 on the basis of the British Carden Lloyd, it is badly outclassed by proper tanks, yet it is still widely deployed as part of infantry and cavalry companies."
TKS	"The TKS tankette is an advanced version of the TK-3 designed in 1933, with an improved hull, suspension, wider tracks, and a Polish-built Fiat engine."
TKS (20mm)	"The TKS tankette is an advanced version of the TK-3 designed in 1933, with an improved hull, suspension, and a Polish-built Fiat engine. About two dozen were armed with a 20mm autocannon, allowing them to present a genuine threat to contemporary armoured vehicles."
R35 (POL)	"A successor to Renault FT, the R35 was designed as a light infantry support tank. It's well-protected, yet slow to move and retains the outdated weaponry of its predecessor. Poland had ordered 100 units to supplement its 7TP tanks, but only 50 were delivered by September 1939."
H39 (POL)	"The Hotchkiss H39 improved upon the H35 with a more reliable and capable engine, but it is still sluggish due to its comparatively heavy armour and very narrow tracks. A test series of three tanks were exported to Poland in July 1939."
Vickers 6-Ton Type A	"First built in 1928, the Vickers Mark E was designed in Britain but all production models were licensed and built abroad. This is a Type A model, with twin turrets mounting a machine gun in each."
Vickers 6-Ton Mark E	"First built in 1928, the Vickers Mark E was designed in Britain but all production models were licensed and built abroad. This is a Type B model, with a single turret mounting a short-barreled 47mm gun."
7TP	"Like the Soviet T-26, the 7TP (7-tonne Polish) design was a more advanced and better armed evolution of the British Vickers Mark E. It is, however, still lightly armoured and has a weaker engine than many of its contemporaries. Around 160 models were available by September 1939, including the early-production models and its uparmoured variant."
7TP dw	"This was the early dual-turret version of the Polish 7TP tank. The main differences between it and the Vickers 6-Ton Mark A are that it features a more powerful and reliable engine, has a high-calibre machine gun in one of the turrets, and includes a radio set in commander vehicles."
VAU-33 (early)	"First built in 1928, the Vickers Mark E was designed in Britain but all production models were licensed and built abroad. This is an early production model VAU-33, which would later go on to become the 7TP."
VAU-33 (late)	"First built in 1928, the Vickers Mark E was designed in Britain but all production models were licensed and built abroad. This is a later production model VAU-33, which would later go on to become the 7TP."
9TP	"The 9TP (9-tonne Polish) design was an up-armoured version of the 7TP design, itself based off the British Vickers Mark E. Only a small number of models were available by the outbreak of the Second World War."
Fiat 508 Taczanka	"The Taczanka was developed as a mobile machine gun platform. This version has a heavy machine gun mounted on the back of a Fiat 508 truck."
wz. 20 Peugeot	"These WW1-era French vehicles were the first armoured cars bought by independent Poland. By 1939, they were long obsolete and were only used by the police force. Nevertheless, several were still deployed in an attempt to combat the German invasion."
wz. 28 (37mm)	"The wz. 28 was an early Polish armoured car, inspired by the half-tracked French armoured cars of the 1920s. The half-tracked design proved difficult to maintain and was a poor fit with the vehicle's underpowered engine, so the production of this design was soon stopped in favour of the more conventional wz. 34. This variant is armed with a French 37mm gun."
wz. 28 (MG)	"The wz. 28 was an early Polish armoured car, inspired by the half-tracked French armoured cars of the 1920s. This variant is armed with a 7.92mm Hotchkiss machine gun."
