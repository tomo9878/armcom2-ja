Ford F15 (ITA)	"The Ford F15 was one of the so-called Military Pattern trucks produced in Canada to outfit British and Commonwealth forces throughout World War 2. Many of these trucks were captured and redeployed by the Italian troops in North Africa."
Tatra 93C	"At the outbreak of the war, Romanian military doctrine did not call for a small, off-road capable command car. Instead, the six-wheeled, all-wheel drive Tatra 93C light truck was used to carry larger groups of officers, in an approach which had soon proved insufficient for the realities of the battlefield."
Praga RV 2-ton (ROM)	"5500 Praga RVs were manufactured in Czechoslovakia between 1935 and 1939. A third of these vehicles were exported abroad, some as far as Peru and Iran. Ultimately, it was the Kingdom of Romania which made the most of them."
Tatra 93R	"Around 800 Czechoslovakian Tatra 93 trucks were produced for the Romanian army before the war. Around half were used as command vehicles, while the rest were of the reconnaissance modification, intended to carry small teams."
Fiat 621 (ROM)	"Produced under license from the Italian Fiat company, the 621 serves as the workhorse of the Polish military. In September 1939, some Polish soldiers escaped over the border to Romania in these trucks, which were subsequently used by the Romanian military."
Tatra 111 (ROM)	"This heavy truck was produced by the Czechoslovakian manufacturer Tatra. Relatively few were produced during the war, but it was extensively manufactured in the aftermath. The first production vehicles were sent to the Hungarian and Romanian armies in 1942. By 1943, it was clear that the engine and transmission were unreliable, and these issues were addressed before its adoption by the Wehrmacht."
Fiat 508 (HUN)	"The Fiat 508 was a highly popular Italian passenger car of the 1930s. Its production run included an off-road capable version designed as a staff car for the Italian army. This unit represents both the vehicles which were exported to Hungary from Italy and the licenced Polski Fiats."
SPA Dovunque 35 (HUN)	"Dovunque means 'Anywhere', as this light Italian truck was specifically designed to be able to travel off-road in North Africa. Some of these vehicles were also exported to Hungary."
Alfa Romeo 800 (HUN)	"The Royal Hungarian Army was one of the least mechanized forces in Europe, and the armoured 38M Botond Truck was not produced in sufficient numbers to cover its needs. These heavy trucks were subsequently imported from Italy, and though less advanced, they still contributed to making up the shortfall."
Traction Avant (BEL)	"This was the general name given to several fast passenger cars created by Citroen before the war, referring to their then-innovative front-wheel drive. While not designed to go off-road, its resilient body and advanced suspension nevertheless made it a suitable staff car. Some were used by Belgium."
Ford M-H (APC)	"The Ford Marmon-Herrington was an early, unarmoured predecessor to the armoured M3 half-tracks used by the Americans during the war. By the end of 1930s, they were written off in the US, but the Belgian military had licensed the chassis, and they modified it to produce this all-wheel drive, armoured gun tractor. It wassometimes used to transport troops."
Citroen-Kegresse P19(BEL)	"This French military half-tracked vehicle was developed in 1932 to transport small teams. The Belgian army used these vehicles as off-road staff cars."
PAG Trekker	"This vehicle was designed as a small, off-road capable troop transport for the Dutch Army. A few vehicles were exported abroad."
Citroen 23 (BEL)	"This light truck was originally produced for the civilian market, and was widely exported abroad. One of its customers was the Belgian military."
37mm wz. 16	"This was the light field support gun developed by Puteaux during the First World War, designed to help infantry combat machine gun nests, and which was later adapted for the Renault FT and the subsequent light tanks. Some of these pieces were exported to Poland, and were deployed in 1939."
37mm wz. 36	"Designed by the Swedish arms manufacturer Bofors, this 37mm anti-tank gun was used by many different forces during the war. It is highly effective against armoured cars and lightly-armoured tanks. They were manufactured under license in Poland, and by 1939, 1200 pieces were in service."
75mm wz. 02/26	"Based on the Russian Putilov 76.2mm gun, this artillery piece has been rechambered to fire 75mm French ammunition, including a small number of armour-piercing shells."
75mm wz. 97	"This artillery gun was the first modern artillery piece, and provided the model for most artillery pieces of the early twentieth century. It saw extended use during the Great War, and thousands were still in service at the start of the Second World War, with around 1,300 used by Poland. It has a small stock of AP shells."
75mm wz. 36	"This long-barreled anti-aircraft gun was a Polish design from Starachowice Works. While highly promising, only around 50 pieces were available at the time of the German invasion, with many deployed at remote static emplacements."
75mm wz. 97/25	"This French-designed anti-aircraft gun was produced in several similar variants. Only around 100 units were available in Poland."
100mm wz. 14/19	"These howitzers were originally built by Skoda for the Austro-Hungarian army during the Great War. After the break-up of the Austro-Hungarian empire, many guns were left with the new Polish state."
105mm wz. 29	"Designed in France, this 105mm gun was both imported and produced under license in Poland. Around 250 pieces were available in 1939, usually assigned to heavy artillery batteries."
120mm wz. 78-31	"This is a modernized version of a French field gun from 1878. It was upgraded with a new barrel, a gun shield, and even a recoil system, although overall it is less effective than purpose-built guns. In 1939, 43 were in service."
155mm wz. 17	"This WW1-era French heavy field howitzer was a widely used and exported design, and some were manufactured under license as well. Poland possessed 340 pieces in August 1939."
220mm wz. 32	"This super-heavy Skoda howitzer was designed in 1928, and 27 pieces were exported to Poland in 1935."
40mm wz. 36	"This is the Polish designation for the 40mm anti-aircraft gun produced by the Swedish arms manufacturer AB Bofors. Around 350 were available in 1939."
PaK 35/36	"First developed in the early 1930s, this was the main anti-tank gun of German forces up to the middle of the war. From March 1943 some were equipped with the Stielgranate 41 HEAT round, which attached to the muzzle of the gun."
3.7cm IG 152(f)	"This is the German designation for captured Puteaux light field support guns. Only a few were used due to the rapidly declining utility of 37mm munitions and the abundance of PaK 35/36 guns. "
4.5cm PaK 184(r)	"This was the German designation for captured Soviet 45mm anti-tank guns. Large numbers were captured, and some were even transferred to occupied France."
PaK 38	"First produced in 1939, this new anti-tank gun was based on the experience of the Spanish Civil War."
