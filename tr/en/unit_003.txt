Wespe	"First deployed on the Eastern Front in the summer of 1943, the Wespe was a self-propelled variant of the 10.5cm leFh. It served in German armoured artillery battalions on all frontlines until the end of the war."
GSW IV B	"This was an assault gun that carried a 10.5 cm leFH 18 howitzer on a modified Panzer IV B chassis. Only ten of these vehicles were produced and they took part in the invasion of the USSR as part of the Army Group South, with the last vehicles lost in the Battle of Stalingrad."
GSW 39H(f) Wurfrahmen	"After taking over France, the Germans ended up with a surplus of H39 tanks in good working condition but of limited combat utility, so many had their turrets removed and were modified to carry other armaments. The most dangerous modification carried four Wurfrahmen 280mm rocket launcher tubes. Only a few were ever produced, and all of them were deployed in Normandy."
GSW Mk VI 736(e)	"Dozens of the British Mk VI light tanks were captured after the Battle of France. Some were converted into makeshift assault guns and used to provide artillery support to Army Group North during Operation Barbarossa."
GSW 37L(f) (105mm)	"Also known as GSW Lorraine Schlepper(f). While most conversions of this French tractor mounted the 7.5cm PaK 40 to recreate the original Marder tank destroyer, some also carried heavier howitzers. These 105mm howitzers were only used in France, fighting between D-Day and the defeat at the Falaise Pocket. "
GSW 37L(f) (150mm)	"Also known as GSW Lorraine Schlepper(f). While most conversions of this trophy French tractor mounted the 7.5cm PaK 40 to recreate the original Marder tank destroyer, some also carried the heavier howitzers, like the 150mm leFH 18. Over 100 tractors were converted into these self-propelled guns, and they were deployed at El Alamein, in the USSR, and in Normandy."
Panzer AMR 35(f) (81mm)	"The AMR 35 was a light, fast cavalry tank of the French army. After their victory, Germany used most of these tanks for police duty across occupied Europe, but some had their turrets removed and converted into mortar carriers."
Panzerjager Tiger	"The Panzerjäger Tiger was converted from Porsche-built Tigers. Known as the Elefant, it was designed as a tank destroyer, but in combat often found itself in an assault gun role."
Panzerjager Tiger (Late)	"Half of Panzerjager Tigers were lost at Kursk, often due to coordinated infantry assaults on isolated vehicles, and all remaining models were refitted with hull MGs as the result. This conversion was known as the Elefant, and it was the most dreaded vehicle on the Eastern Front, with Soviet reports overestimating their abundance by up to 20 times. 11 out of 48 models were also deployed in Italy."
Krupp Protze (37mm)	"The Krupp Protze was extensively produced before the war as a tractor for light anti-tank guns and anti-aircraft guns. By 1939, many units had mounted their PaK 36 guns directly onto the flatbed. However, it fared poorly compared to half-tracks and the more specialized tank destroyers, and it gradually fell out of favour after the Polish Campaign."
Sd. Kfz. 10 (37mm)	"Sd. Kfz. 10 light half-tracks were often used to tow PaK 35/36 AT guns, and some crews went further and mounted the gun in its rear compartment. This early field modification did not even remove the wheels and the split tail of the original gun, allowing for it to be removed if needed, but also limiting its traverse atop the vehicle."
8.8cm FlaK 18 Sfl.	"The 8.8cm FlaK 18 and its variants were found effective as an anti-tank weapon. In 1939 the gun was mounted on Sd.Kfz. 8 and 9 half-tracked transporters. They were used in small numbers during the invasion of Poland, France and on the southern sector of the Eastern Front."
Marder II	"Built upon a Panzer II chassis, the Marder was developed as a makeshift tank destroyer, initially for countering Soviet tanks.This variant has light armour but a powerful 75mm main gun."
Marder II D	"By 1942, it was clear that heavy tanks would only become more numerous on the battlefield, yet few of the Wehrmacht's vehicles could counter them at range. The surplus of captured Soviet 76.2mm guns allowed for their placement on 150 hulls of now-obsolete Panzer IIs in the Spring, with 50 more Marder IIs completed by 1943. This tank destroyer was capable but excessively tall, making it an easy target."
Marder II (50mm)	"While the Marder II tank destroyer was intended to mount either a PaK 40 or a captured Soviet 76.2mm gun, this wasn't always possible. In June 1943, several Panzer IIs were modified in the field to carry a 50mm PaK 38 anti-tank gun."
Marder III	"Built upon a Panzer 38(t) chassis, the Marder was developed as a makeshift tank destroyer, initially for countering Soviet tanks. This early variant was equipped with a captured 76.2mm Soviet gun. It was deployed on the Eastern Front and in North Africa."
Marder III H	"Built upon a Panzer 38(t) chassis, the Marder was developed as a makeshift tank destroyer, initially for countering Soviet tanks.This later variant is equipped with a German PaK 40 gun."
Marder III M	"Built upon a Panzer 38(t) chassis, the Marder was developed as a makeshift tank destroyer, initially for countering Soviet tanks.This variant has light armour but a powerful 75mm main gun."
Panzerjager I	"Built upon a Panzer I chassis, this self-propelled AT gun mounts a 4.7cm anti-tank gun."
Jagdpanzer IV (early)	"Intended as a replacement for the StuG, the JgdPz IV is a Tank Destroyer developed in late 1943. It is built upon a modified Panzer IV chassis. This early production model uses thinner plates and possesses both MG apertures."
Jagdpanzer IV	"This main production model uses thicker frontal plates as well as doing away with the muzzle brake. Additionally, MG port on the gunner's side is welded over with an armoured cap."
Jagdpanzer 38(t)	"Built upon a Panzer 38(t) chassis, this Tank Destroyer, commonly known as the Hetzer, was intended to replace the ad-hoc Tank Destroyers of earlier years."
PSW P204(f) (50mm)	"Panzerspahwagen P204 was the German designation for captured French Panhard P-178 armoured cars. By 1944, the 34 P204s stationed in Normandy had been converted into wheeled tank destroyers by replacing their turret with an open mount for a 50mm gun from the older Panzer III tanks. "
PSW 234/4	"This was the tank hunter variant of the PSW 234, and the only model produced after December 1944, when nothing became as important as repelling the massive quantities of Soviet and Allied armour advancing into Germany."
Jagdpanzer IV/70	"This is a Jagdpanzer IV armed with the 7.5cm PaK 42 L/70 gun used by the Panther tank. The result was a powerful but front-heavy tank destroyer. It was the most produced variant of this vehicle."
Jagdpanzer IV/70 (A)	"This is the 'A' variant of the Jagdpanzer IV/70, a tank destroyer armed with a 7.5cm PaK 42 L/70 gun. This variant was an attempt to fit the gun into unmodified Panzer IV hull, and was only produced in small numbers."
Nashorn	"The Nashorn tank destroyer was armed with a powerful 88mm gun, but its light armour made it very vulnerable to enemy fire. Most Nashorns were used to hunt down Soviet tanks. A few were deployed in Italy and Western Europe."
Jagdpanzer V	"This German tank destroyer combines the chassis of the Panzer V (Panther) with the 88mm gun of the King Tiger."
Jagdpanzer VI	"The Jagdtiger was the most heavily armoured tank ever used by the Wehrmacht and the only German vehicle equipped with the 12.8 cm PaK 44 L/55. Because of its excessive weight of more than 70 tonnes, it often suffered from severe technical issues."
Pz.Sfl. V	"This experimental tank destroyer was built on the chassis of a canceled VK 30.01 (H) prototype tank and wields a powerful 128mm cannon. It was more than capable of defeating any enemy armoured vehicle at long distances. Two vehicles were used on the Eastern Front by the Army Group South."
