Caudron-Renault CR.714	"Introduced in early 1940, the Caudron-Renault CR.714 quickly turned out to be a disappointment. Its wooden construction and underpowered engine seriously limited its operational capability. Nevertheless, several dozen fighters of this type were flown by Free Polish pilots during the Battle of France. The CR.714 was armed with four 7.5mm MAC 1934 machine guns."
Loire-Nieuport LN.411	"Introduced in early 1940, the Loire-Nieuport LN.411 dive bomber saw action during the Battle of France. Only a few dozen planes of this type were built and most of them were already lost in May 1940. The LN.411 was armed with a 20mm Hispano-Suiza HS.404 cannon, two 7.5mm Darne machine guns and a bomb payload of up to 225 kg. This unit also represents the similar LN.401 variant."
Machine Gun Turret	"This was the lightest of the static turret emplacements used on the Maginot Line. Similar emplacements were also used in defensive lines elsewhere in the world."
Mixed Arms Turret	"This turret was attached to some Maginot Line bunkers. It is equipped with twinned machine guns and a 25mm cannon. Similar emplacements were also used in defensive lines elsewhere in the world."
FT-17 (MG) Turret	"In 1938, some Spanish Republican T-26s escaped to France. After Colonel De Gaulle insisted on field tests in the Ardennes Forest, it was proven that only contemporary French designs could be deterred by its terrain. Hundreds of turrets were taken from the leftover fleet of over 500 FT-17 tanks and secretly installed there and on the Meuse River as emergency fortifications."
FT-17 (37mm) Turret	"Revolutionary during the Great War, Renault FT-17 moved slower than an infantryman and was unsuited for multi-front warfare. Worse, the gun on FTs was a 37mm trench gun, designed to wear down fortifications rather than to combat armoured vehicles. Thus, cannon-bearing turrets which couldn't be rearmed were instead concealed behind natural obstacles to provide flanking fire against thinner side armour."
FT-17 (25mm) Turret	"Limited production of the 25mm AT guns meant that only some turrets installed in the Ardennes could be rearmed in time. Measures used involved exploiting bottlenecks in AMD 35 production, scaling back supplies of the gun to the BEF and denying them outright to Finland."
FCM 36 Turret	"The FCM-36 infantry support tank was designed to be gas-proof, which led to very high cost for limited performance. Its turret was, however, cheaper than the one on rival R35 and H35 tanks. FCM was paid in advance to resume turret production to reinforce ad hoc fortifications. Their purpose was to destroy infantry and light vehicles while drawing away fire from turrets with better guns, but worse armour."
AMC-35 Turret	"AMC 35 production was badly mismanaged, producing more turrets than hulls. Belgium initially expressed interest in purchasing some turrets as coastal fortifications. Instead, all 15 spare turrets served as centrepieces of ad hoc fortifications at the Ardennes."
Bazooka Team	"First used in 1942, the portable recoilless anti-tank rocket launcher was an American invention and was mainly used by American forces during the war. It launches a high-explosive anti-tank shaped charge."
SBD Dauntless	"The Douglass SBD Dauntless was a scout bomber aircraft manufactured from 1940 to 1944. It saw action in the South Pacific where it undertook dive bombing attacks on Japanese ships."
F4F Wildcat	"The Grumman F4F Wildcat was a carrier-based fighter, and was the main fighter aircraft in American Navy and Marine service during the early part of the war."
P-40 Warhawk	"The Curtiss P-40 Warhawk was designed in late 1930s, and played an important role in American action in North Africa and in Southeast Asia."
P-47 Thunderbolt	"The Republic P-47 Thunderbolt was one of the main fighter aircraft used by American forces during the war."
PIAT Team	"Developed from rifle-mounted AT grenades, the Projector, Infantry, Anti Tank (PIAT) fires a shaped charge by means of a heavy spring."
Hurricane Mk I	"The Hawker Hurricane was one of the most numerous British and Commonwealth fighters in World War Two. The Mk I variant was armed with eight 7.7mm Browning machine guns."
Hurricane Mk IIB	"The Hawker Hurricane was one of the most numerous British and Commonwealth fighters in World War Two. The Mk IIB variant was armed with twelve 7.7mm Browning machine guns and was able to carry two 250- or 500-pound bombs. It was also provided to the Soviet Union through the Lend-Lease programme."
Hurricane Mk IIC	"The Hawker Hurricane was one of the most numerous British and Commonwealth fighters in World War Two. The Mk IIC variant was a dedicated fighter-bomber, equipped with four 20mm Hispano Mk II cannons and two 250- or 500-pound bombs."
Hurricane Mk IID	"The Hawker Hurricane was one of the most numerous British and Commonwealth fighters in World War Two. The Mk IID variant was a dedicated tank hunter, equipped with two 40mm Rolls Royce or Vickers S cannons and a single 7.7mm Browning machine gun."
Typhoon	"The Hawker Typhoon was introduced in 1941 as a replacement for the Hurricane."
Tempest	"The Hawker Tempest was an improved version of the Typhoon, with a more powerful engine and better performance at high altitudes."
Spitfire Mk I/II	"The Supermarine Spitfire was one of the most popular British fighters in World War 2. This unit represents the early variants, most of which were equipped with eight 7.7mm Browning machine guns."
Spitfire Mk V	"The Supermarine Spitfire was one of the most popular British fighters in World War 2. This unit represents the Mk V variants, most of which were armed with four 7.7mm Browning machine guns, two 20mm Hispano II cannons and a bomb payload of up to 500 pounds.Some Spitfires of this type were also provided to the Soviet Union through the Lend-Lease programme."
Spitfire Mk IX	"The Supermarine Spitfire was one of the most popular British fighters in World War 2. This unit represents the Mk IX (and similar) variants, most of which were armed with two 7.7mm Browning machine guns, two 20mm Hispano II cannons and a bomb payload of up to 1000 pounds. Some Spitfires of this type were also provided to the Soviet Union through the Lend-Lease programme."
Fokker C.X	"The Fokker C.X was a biplane designed and built in the Netherlands. The Finnish army used them for reconnaisance and as light bombers."
Fiat G.50 (FIN)	"The Fiat G.50 fighter was Italy's first single-seat, all-metal monoplane with an enclosed cockpit and a retractable landing gear. It was exported to several other nations, including Finland. In Finnish service, the G.50 saw action against the Red Army during the Winter War and Continuation War."
MS.406 (FIN)	"Introduced in 1939, the Morane-Saulnier MS.406 was the most numerous French fighter during World War Two. Almost 100 planes of this type were exported or delivered to Finland and deployed against the Red Army in the Winter and Continuation Wars. The MS.406 was armed with a 20mm Hispano-Suiza HS.404 cannon and two 7.5mm MAC 1934 machine guns."
MS.410 (FIN)	"The Morane-Saulnier MS.410 was developed from the MS.406. It received a stronger wing and two additional 7.5mm machine guns among other improvements. A small number of MS.410 fighters was delivered to Finland and served in the Winter and Continuation Wars."
Morko-Morane	"The Morko-Morane was a modified Finnish version of the French Morane-Saulnier MS.406. It was equipped with a much more powerful, Soviet engine along with other improvements. Most Morko-Moranes were armed with four machine guns and a single 20mm cannon. They saw limited action in the Continuation and Lapland Wars."
