KV-8S	"This is a flamethrower variant of the KV-1S, which replaces the 76mm main gun with a 45mm one in order to mount a co-axial flamethrower in the turret. There is an external cover mimicking the old barrel dimensions to prevent easy identification by the enemy. 32 were produced."
KV-2	"Designed to destroy fortifications, the KV-2 is unusual in that it mounts its heavy gun in a very large and very distinctive rotatable turret."
KV-85	"This was a stopgap model that combined the turret from the prototype IS-1 with the hull of the KV-1S. Only 130 were ever produced."
IS-1	"Developed as an answer to the German Tiger and Panther, the Iosef Stalin 1, or IS-1 (also called IS-85) received additional armour compared to the KV-1 as well as the new D-5T 85mm gun, which soon was considered too weak for a heavy tank. One of the 7.62mm machine guns was placed in the turret rear."
IS-2 (early)	"The Iosef Stalin 2, or IS-2 (also called IS-122) gradually replaced the IS-1 (or IS-85) over the course of 1944. It was armed with the D-25T 122mm gun, which offered impressive firepower but was difficult to reload and limited the ammunition capacity of the tank stores to only 28 shells. One of the 7.62mm machine guns was placed in the turret rear."
IS-2 (late)	"The Iosef Stalin 2, or IS-2 (also called IS-122) gradually replaced the IS-1 (or IS-85) over the course of 1944. It was armed with the D-25T 122mm gun, which offered impressive firepower but was difficult to reload and limited the ammunition capacity of the tank stores to only 28 shells. This late variant was equipped with an additional 12.7mm MG and had additional armour."
ZiS-30	"The ZiS-30 was an improvised tank destroyer based on the Komsomolets armoured artillery tractor. About 100 of them were produced and deployed in response to the German invasion of the Soviet Union. Its 57mm ZiS-2 gun turned out to be very effective against German armour. However, it was mounted on an unreliable and poorly-armoured vehicle, and the crew is exposed to enemy fire."
SU-4	"The SU-4 was a rare assault gun which mounted an experimental recoilless 76mm gun onto a modified truck with a motorized flatbed, allowing it to fire in all directions. Its weapon was powerful but too complicated for mass production, and only a few were deployed during the Winter War and in 1941."
SU-5	"The SU-5 mounted a 122mm howitzer atop a T-26 tank chassis. The weight of the gun had further burdened the chassis, and only 8 shells could be carried, which rendered this design ineffective. Around 30 were produced, and all were lost by the end of 1941. "
SU-14	"The SU-14 was a super-heavy assault gun based on the T-35 chassis. It was at first intended to carry the 'Karelian Sculptor' 203mm gun to support the offensive on the Mannerheim Line, but they did not reach the frontline until the war was over. By 1941, both prototypes were redesigned to carry the long-barreled 152mmm Br-2 gun, and provided artillery support during the Battle of Moscow."
SU-26	"The SU-26 was a conversion of damaged T-26 tanks to carry an obr. 27 76mm field gun. Unlike most such conversions, it had a rotating turret, and even a pair of machine guns. There were, however, no turret hatches outside of the open rear and the design was extremely cramped. These conversions were only performed around Leningrad, on around 30 vehicles, the last of which fought until summer 1944."
SU-85	"The SU-85 was designed to destroy the latest German tanks in 1943, and featured an 85mm anti-aircraft gun mounted on to an SU-122."
SU-85M	"Introduced in late 1944, this variant of the SU-85 tank destroyer received a different gun mantlet with improved armour and a new commander vision cupola."
SU-100	"By the end of 1944, 85mm guns were no longer considered powerful enough for Soviet tank destroyers. Because of this, the SU-100 was introduced in 1945. It was armed with the new D-10S 100mm gun and equipped with a commander vision cupola."
SU-100Y	"In 1940 one of the prototypes of the T-100 tank was rebuilt into a self-propelled gun armed with a 130mm naval cannon mounted in large superstructure. This SU-100Y was used during the defense of Moscow in Kubinka area. Little is known about its later service if any."
BA-3	"The 'Broneavtomobil 3' entered production in 1933 and mounted an adapted T-26 turret with the same armament. No other armoured car at the time was so heavily armed, but it had a poor suspension and was prone to overheating, so only 220 were built before the introduction of the BA-6."
BA-3 (half-track)	"The BA-3 could be turned into a makeshift half-track by attaching 'Overall' tracks to its rear wheels, which was done during winter engagements."
BA-6	"This heavy armoured car is armed with a 45mm gun."
BA-6 (half-track)	"The BA-6 was capable of being converted to a half-track through attaching 'Overall' tracks to its rear wheels, which was done during winter deployments."
BA-10	"The BA-10 improved upon the BA-6 with some enhancements to armour and a much more powerful engine. Nearly 3000 were produced in total."
BA-10 (half-track)	"Like its predecessors, the BA-10 could be converted to a makeshift half-track through attaching 'Overall' tracks to its rear wheels, which was done during winter deployments."
BA-11	"This was the final Soviet heavy armoured car design. Unlike its predecessors, it used a more powerful ZIS-6 chassis, which allowed it to move faster while carrying more ammunition. The lack of all-wheel drive, however, compromised its off-road mobility, and all attempts to develop one were interrupted by the German invasion. Only around 20 were made, most deployed in the defence of Ukraine."
BA-20	"This armoured car was designed upon a Ford model A chassis and uses a modified Model T engine."
BA-27	"The BA-27 was one of the earliest Soviet armoured cars, produced between 1928 and 1931. By 1941 they were obsolete and badly worn out, yet some were still assigned to reconnaisance groups near the border. By autumn, every remaining armoured car was brought out of storage, at times deployed in frontline combat."
BA-64	"This armoured car was designed upon the GAZ 4x4 automobile. Its sloped armour allowed for superior protection compared to earlier designs while maintaining high speed. The production of this initial model was rushed due to the German invasion and it was both unreliable and very uncomfortable."
BA-64B	"This armoured car was designed upon the GAZ 4x4 automobile."
BA-I	"The 'Broneavtomobil Izhorsky' was the first Soviet armoured car to use a six-wheeled chassis. It is relatively fast, but poorly armoured and the engine radiates hot air straight into the crew compartment. It was soon replaced by the BA-3 and its successors on the GAZ-AAA chassis. The 110 finished units were used for training up until Operation Barbarossa, when they were hurriedly deployed and were lost by September."
PB (45mm)	"After the summer of 1941, the vast majority of prewar Soviet armour was destroyed or abandoned in disrepair. Before the production of new designs reached full capacity, improvised vehicles had to fill the gap. This included the PB, an uparmoured ZIS-5 truck which was produced by Leningrad factories to defend against German and Finnish troops. This was the more common variant, armed with a 45mm gun."
PB (20mm)	"This less common variant of the PB carried a 20mm ShVAK autocannon."
FAI	"FAI stands for Ford A Izhorsky, as it was designed in Izhorsk and used a Ford model A chassis. Its off-road capability was very poor, yet 700 of these armoured cars were produced in the early 1930s before the adoption of the BA-20."
